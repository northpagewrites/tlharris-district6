Early Literacy

Not Started

Early Literacy and Third-Grade Reading

Third-grade reading proficiency across Memphis-Shelby County Schools, and what the Board asks about it.

“Increase third-grade reading proficiency by 10% within two academic years.”2026 campaign pledge — historical context, not a current commitment

He directly controls: How he votes on items that come before the Board. What he asks for in Board meetings and puts on the public record. What he reports back to District 6.

Depends on the district: Reading outcomes depend on classroom instruction, school leadership and district-wide programs. The Board acts as a body, and any single member's vote is one of nine.

Not Started

Early Literacy Instruction and Structured Literacy

How reading is taught in the early grades, and which instructional approach the district uses.

“Expand structured literacy practices across all K-3 classrooms.”2026 campaign pledge — historical context, not a current commitment

He directly controls: How he votes on curriculum and budget items that reach the Board. What he asks about instructional practice in public session.

Depends on the district: Curriculum selection and teacher training are run by district administration. A Board member does not set classroom practice.

Student Achievement

Not Started

Early Intervention

How quickly students who fall behind are identified, and what support follows.

“Prioritize early intervention before grade retention becomes necessary.”2026 campaign pledge — historical context, not a current commitment

He directly controls: How he votes on programs and funding that come before the Board. What he asks about identification and support in public session.

Depends on the district: Identification and support happen at school level, under district programs. Some requirements come from outside the district.

Not Started

School Improvement Goals

School improvement goals: what they are, who sets them, and how the Board oversees progress against them.

“Require every District 6 school to publish annual measurable improvement goals.”2026 campaign pledge — historical context, not a current commitment

Improvement goals are how a district states publicly what it is trying to change.

He directly controls: How he votes on goals and plans presented to the Board. What he asks for in the way of progress reporting.

Depends on the district: Goals are set and carried out by district and school leadership. The Board's role is oversight, exercised as a body.

Safe & Supportive Schools

Not Started

Safe and Supportive Learning Environments

School climate, behavioral standards, and the support available to students and staff.

“Reduce classroom disruptions by 15% within two years through clear behavioral standards. Every student deserves a learning environment where they feel secure, supported, and ready to learn.”2026 campaign pledge — historical context, not a current commitment

He directly controls: How he votes on policy that comes before the Board. What he raises publicly about school conditions in District 6.

Depends on the district: Day-to-day discipline and safety are handled by school leadership under district policy. A Board member does not manage staff or individual student matters.

Accountability & Stewardship

Not Started

Annual Literacy Growth Reports

Whether literacy growth is reported publicly each year, and in a form residents can find and read.

“Publish annual literacy growth reports for District 6 schools, and host quarterly public updates to ensure transparency and accountability.”2026 campaign pledge — historical context, not a current commitment

A commitment nobody reports on cannot be checked by anyone.

He directly controls: What he asks the district to report. What he publishes to District 6 through this site, whether or not the district publishes its own.

Depends on the district: District-wide reporting is produced by the administration. What this office can do on its own is publish what it has, and say plainly what it does not have.

Not Started

Public Performance Dashboard

Whether district performance information is published in one place the public can find and use.

“Launch a publicly accessible quarterly performance dashboard within the first year of service.”2026 campaign pledge — historical context, not a current commitment

Information the public cannot find cannot hold anyone to account.

He directly controls: What this office publishes and keeps current on this site.

Depends on the district: A district-wide dashboard would be built and maintained by the district. Any figure shown here must come from a published district or state source, with that source named.

Family & Community Engagement

Not Started

Quarterly Town Halls

Regular public meetings in District 6 where residents can raise concerns directly.

“Host quarterly District 6 town halls and publish meeting summaries within 10 business days.”2026 campaign pledge — historical context, not a current commitment

Residents need a predictable place to raise something and be answered in public.

He directly controls: Whether the meetings are scheduled and held. Whether a summary is published afterwards. What follow-up is recorded.

Depends on the district: Holding the meetings is within this office's own control. Attendance, and what the district does in response to what is raised, are not.
